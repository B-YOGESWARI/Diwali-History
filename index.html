Fourteen Years Away

Rama, Sita and Lakshmana are traditionally described as spending fourteen years in exile, away from Ayodhya.

02

Forests and Trials

Their journey is remembered through the Ramayana as a time filled with challenges, encounters and courage.

03

The Journey Home

After exile and the victory over Ravana, they are traditionally believed to have returned towards Ayodhya.

04

Ayodhya Awaits

People of Ayodhya welcomed Rama by lighting rows of oil lamps throughout the city.

05

Light Over Darkness

The lamp became one of the most recognisable symbols of Diwali — light, hope and renewal.

And the story became a tradition. But Diwali is more than one story.

Small Choices, Brighter Diwali

Celebrating with a Little More Thought

Diwali has always been about bringing light into our lives. Today, we can carry that idea a little further — by celebrating in ways that are kinder to the people around us, to animals, and to the environment.

🪔

Celebrate with Light

Diyas, candles and beautiful decorations are at the heart of Diwali. Using them thoughtfully and safely can make the celebration just as beautiful without creating unnecessary waste.

🌿

Let the Celebration Breathe

Fireworks are part of how many people celebrate, but keeping them to a minimum can make the festival more comfortable for children, older people, people who are unwell, and those who are sensitive to noise.

🐾

Remember Our Four-Legged Friends

The sounds of Diwali can be frightening for animals. Giving pets and nearby animals a quiet, comfortable place during the celebrations is a small thing that can make a big difference.

♻️

Leave Less Behind

Choose decorations that can be reused, avoid unnecessary plastic, and take a little time to clean up after the celebrations. A beautiful festival deserves a clean morning after it.

🍱

Share the Happiness

Diwali food is meant to be shared. Instead of preparing more than we need, we can share sweets and meals with neighbors, friends, colleagues, or someone who may be celebrating alone.

❤️

Make Room for Everyone

Not everyone celebrates Diwali in the same way. Being considerate of our neighbors and including people around us can make the festival feel warmer for everyone.

👨‍👩‍👧

Celebrate Safely

Keep diyas, candles and fireworks away from anything that can catch fire, and make sure children are supervised. A little care helps everyone enjoy the celebration without worry.

🛍️

Support Local Hands

Buying diyas, decorations and handmade products from local artisans can add something special to the celebration while supporting the people who keep these traditional crafts alive.

💰

Keep It Meaningful

Diwali doesn't have to be about spending more. Sometimes the simplest celebrations — a home filled with diyas, good food and people we love — become the memories we remember most.

Perhaps celebrating Diwali responsibly doesn't mean celebrating less. It simply means celebrating with a little more awareness, a little more kindness, and a little more thought for the world around us.

Because the brightest Diwali is not only the one that lights up our homes — it's the one that brings a little more light into someone else's life.
