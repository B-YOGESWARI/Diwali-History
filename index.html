Fourteen Years Away

Rama, Sita and Lakshmana are traditionally described as spending fourteen years in exile, away from Ayodhya.

02

Forests and Trials

Their journey is remembered through the Ramayana as a time filled with challenges, encounters and courage.

03

The Journey Home

After exile and the victory over Ravana, they are traditionally believed to have returned towards Ayodhya.

04

Ayodhya Awaits

People of Ayodhya welcomed Rama by lighting rows of oil lamps throughout the city.

05

Light Over Darkness

The lamp became one of the most recognisable symbols of Diwali — light, hope and renewal.

And the story became a tradition. But Diwali is more than one story.

🪔

Lighting Diyas

Oil lamps fill homes, entrances and prayer spaces with warm light.

🌸

Rangoli

Patterns of powders and flowers decorate entrances and courtyards.

🙏

Prayer & Lakshmi Puja

Families seek blessings, prosperity and well-being for the year ahead.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Family & Food

Gifts, sweets and shared meals become the memories we keep.

Light, togetherness and renewal remain common themes across regions.

Diwali Today

The Celebration Continues

Modern Diwali can still fill our homes with light, food and togetherness — while we stay considerate of the air we share, the people around us, animals who hear the noise, and the morning that follows the celebration.

Explore Today ↓
Understanding the impact

What the celebration can leave behind

Joy does not have to come at the cost of the air, quiet nights, or a clean street the next day.

🌫️

Smoke & Air

Fireworks can raise fine particles; monitoring studies record festival-period spikes that make the air harder to breathe for a while.

❤️

Health & Noise

Smoke and loud sounds can be hard on children, elders, animals and anyone with sensitive lungs or hearing.

♻️

What Remains

Burnt paper and cracker waste can linger long after the celebration ends — on streets, rooftops and in shared spaces.

Small Choices, Brighter Diwali

Celebrating with a Little More Thought

Diwali has always been about bringing light into our lives. Today, we can carry that idea a little further — by celebrating in ways that feel a bit kinder to the people around us, to animals, and to the world we share.

🪔

Celebrate with Light

Diyas and candles are what many of us remember most about Diwali. Lighting them with a little care — and letting them burn safely — can make the night just as beautiful, without leaving a pile of waste behind.

🌿

Let the Celebration Breathe

Fireworks are part of the fun for many families. Even so, a gentler approach — fewer crackers, quieter moments — can make the evening easier for children, elders, anyone who is unwell, and neighbours who prefer a softer night.

🐾

Remember Our Four-Legged Friends

Loud sounds can scare pets and street animals. Offering them a quiet room, soft music, or a familiar corner during the celebrations is a small kindness that often means more than we realise.

♻️

Leave Less Behind

Choosing decorations we can reuse, skipping extra plastic, and taking a few minutes to tidy up the next morning helps the festival feel complete — not only beautiful the night of, but clean the day after.

🍱

Share the Happiness

Sweets and meals are meant to be shared. Passing a box to a neighbour, inviting someone who might be alone, or simply cooking a little less than “too much” keeps the joy circulating beyond our own table.

❤️

Make Room for Everyone

Not every home celebrates the same way. A little consideration for neighbours, and an open door for people who might feel left out, can make the whole street feel warmer.

👨‍👩‍👧

Celebrate Safely

Keeping flames and fireworks away from curtains and dry leaves, and staying close when children are around them, is simply looking after each other so everyone can enjoy the night without worry.

🛍️

Support Local Hands

Diyas, rangoli colours and decorations from local makers carry a different kind of warmth. Choosing them supports the people who keep these crafts alive, and often makes our own celebration feel more personal.

💰

Keep It Meaningful

Diwali does not need to cost more every year. A quiet evening with diyas, good food and people we love is often what we remember longest — long after the shopping bags are gone.

🪔

Small Choices, Brighter Diwali

Celebrate the joy. Protect the air. Keep the light alive.

Celebrating Diwali with a little more care does not mean celebrating less. It simply means noticing the people, animals and shared spaces around us — and letting that awareness guide how we light up the night.

Because the brightest Diwali is not only the one that lights up our homes — it is the one that brings a little more light into someone else’s life.
